# P16: the twelve base integration-gate failures

`pnpm test:integration` on `me` at `a9fe0cc` failed 12 tests in 4 files. All twelve
share one machine shape: this machine's Postgres runs `Asia/Dubai` (+04) where the
CI container runs UTC, and four places mixed two conventions for naive
`timestamp(3)` columns. Prisma writes `DateTime` columns as the UTC wall clock
(its driver adapter sends `Date` as a UTC ISO string, and Postgres keeps the
literal fields of a naive column), and reads them back the same way. Anything
that instead lets Postgres compare or cast against a `timestamptz` clock, or
sends a `Date` through a raw pg client, reads or writes the session's TimeZone
wall clock instead: four hours off here, exactly right on UTC CI.

Two failures were product defects (the product refused valid states); two were
test fixtures that wrote instants the column convention does not carry.

## 1. `push-token-store.postgres.test.ts`: 8 failures, "Push session ended"

**Product defect.** Both registration paths compare the session's expiry with
bare `CURRENT_TIMESTAMP`:

    SELECT id FROM "session" WHERE ... AND "expiresAt" > CURRENT_TIMESTAMP FOR UPDATE

`expiresAt` is a naive timestamp holding a UTC instant; `CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` is a
`timestamptz`. Postgres re-interprets the naive value in the session's TimeZone,
so on +04 a session with an hour left read as four hours expired and every push
registration, import and handover threw `PushSessionEndedError`. On a UTC
server the two sides agree and the suite passes; on a deployment whose database
zone is ahead of UTC the product refuses every valid registration. The one
passing test was the one that expects the error for a deliberately expired
session, which is why the suite read as "always ends".

**Fix** (`packages/adapters/src/push-token-store.ts`, both raw queries): compare
against `(CURRENT_TIMESTAMP AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')`, the same UTC wall clock the
column carries. No observable change on a UTC server.

**Guard** (`push-token-store.postgres.test.ts`): "registers a fresh session on
a database whose TimeZone is ahead of UTC" creates its own pool pinned to
`options=-c timezone=Pacific/Kiritimati` (UTC+14, the widest ahead-of-UTC shift)
and requires a session one hour from expiry to register and to load back.

**Mutation, named: "bare CURRENT_TIMESTAMP restored in register()"**. With the
old comparison back in `register()`:

    × registers a fresh session on a database whose TimeZone is ahead of UTC 52ms
    ⎯ Failed Tests 9 (the guard, plus the original 8)

With the guard restored (diff empty): 10 passed (10).

## 2. `media-video-poll.postgres.test.ts`: 2 failures, missing `media.poll` effect

**Test fixture defect.** The `stall` helper, which fakes a job whose scheduled
poll was lost, wrote `"updatedAt" = now() - interval '15 minutes'` through raw
SQL. `now()` is a `timestamptz`; the cast to the naive column stores the
session-local wall clock. Probe on this server (`TimeZone = Asia/Dubai`):

    old_fixture_writes       = 2026-11-18 21:52:06.335781+04   (naive 21:52 stored)
    utc_convention           = 2026-11-18 17:52:06.335781     (what Prisma writes)

Stored four hours ahead of the convention, the stalled job read as fresh and
`reconcileMediaVideos` (`updatedAt < now - 10 minutes`) found nothing, so no
`media.poll` was enqueued and no recovery happened. The product is internally
consistent: every write to the column goes through Prisma, and the recovery
query compares UTC-naive against a UTC-naive parameter. On UTC CI the fixture
coincidentally wrote the right value.

**Fix** (the fixture): `(now() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC') - interval '15 minutes'`,
with a comment naming the convention. **Mutation: the old fixture itself.**
Before the fix: 2 failed (the two reconcile assertions, `[]` received); after:
14 passed (14). No new product guard was needed; the two failing tests are the
guard.

## 3. `unread-inbox-chats.postgres.test.ts`: 1 failure, 8 unread against 7

**Product defect.** `countUnreadInboxChats` (`packages/db/src/unread-inbox-chats.ts`)
filters snoozed threads with `r."snoozedUntil" <= now()`: a naive column
holding UTC instants against a `timestamptz`. On +04, a chat snoozed an hour
into the future read as already woken and counted as unread, so the count was
8 instead of 7. Symmetrically, a snooze that had just passed read as woken four
hours earlier than it was. On a UTC server the test passes; the product
miscounts snoozes on any ahead-of-UTC deployment.

**Fix**: `<= (now() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')`, comment naming why.

**Guard** ("keeps a snoozed chat out of the count on a database whose TimeZone
is ahead of UTC"): pins a UTC+14 pool, adds one still-snoozed chat and one
woken chat on top of whatever the earlier test left, and asserts the count
rises by exactly 1 on both the pinned client and the normal one, so it does not
depend on test order.

**Mutation, named: "bare now() restored in the snooze predicate"**:

    FAIL ... > matches the inbox across member Spaces, including chats other members made
    AssertionError: expected 8 to be 7
    FAIL ... > keeps a snoozed chat out of the count on a database whose TimeZone is ahead of UTC
    AssertionError: expected 10 to be 9

(the future snooze also counted). With the guard restored: 2 passed (2).

## 4. `schedule-timezone.postgres.test.ts`: 1 failure, `2026-11-16` against `2026-11-23`

**Test fixture defect, and the date fixture was right.** The "one week apart,
depends on the day the suite runs" reading in the P15 notes was the symptom,
not the cause. The claim barrier's raw pg client passed the claimed instant as
a JS `Date`:

    await client.query('UPDATE routines SET "nextRunAt" = $2 WHERE id = $1', [routine.id, claimed])

pg's `prepareValue` serializes a `Date` as the LOCAL wall clock with an offset
(`2026-11-16T11:00:00.000+04:00` here); Postgres ignores the offset when the
target is a naive column and stored `11:00`, where the Prisma convention is
`07:00` (the UTC wall clock of the same instant). The zone save then read
`due = 2026-11-16T11:00:00Z`, correctly found the pending occurrence's local
time (13:00 Cairo) not carried by the cron, and recomputed the next Monday
09:00 London after the stored instant: `2026-11-23T09:00:00.000Z`. The suite's
frozen clock (`vi.useFakeTimers`, 2026-11-05) was held the whole time; a probe
logged `Date.now() = 2026-11-05T12:00:00.000Z` inside the save transaction.

**Product proven right**: a standalone run of `changeUserTimezone` with the
same frozen clock and a row carrying `nextRunAt = 2026-11-16T07:00:00.000Z`
(what the claim writes under the convention) produced exactly
`2026-11-16T09:00:00.000Z`: the claimed occurrence's local time held.

**Fix** (the fixture): pass `claimed.toISOString()`, the string form Prisma
writes, with a comment naming the pg Date-serialization hazard.
**Mutation: the old Date-parameter fixture itself**: 1 failed before, 5 passed
(5) after.

## Known same-shape hazard, out of scope

`packages/adapters/src/child-bots.ts` writes `"updatedAt" = CURRENT_TIMESTAMP`
into `memory_documents` through raw SQL (the archive path). Same write-direction
mismatch as this packet's fixtures: on a server ahead of UTC it stores the
session-local wall clock, hours ahead of every Prisma write to that column. No
failing test exposes it and no packet file names it, so it is recorded here for
a later packet rather than fixed blind.
